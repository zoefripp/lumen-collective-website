You may have seen "trauma-informed" on a counsellor's website and wondered what it means, or whether it is only for people who have been through something terrible. It is not. Trauma-informed counselling is a way of working that makes sessions safer and more comfortable for everyone.

## What is trauma?

Trauma is the impact an event or series of events can have on a person, not the event itself. Two people can go through the same thing and be affected very differently.

Trauma can come from a single event, like an accident or an assault. It can also build up over time, through things like ongoing stress at home, bullying, discrimination, medical experiences or not having your needs met as a child. Many people carry the effects without ever calling it trauma.

Trauma can show up as feeling on edge, shutting down, struggling to trust people, being easily overwhelmed, or finding it hard to feel safe even when nothing is wrong.

## What makes counselling trauma-informed?

Trauma-informed counselling does not mean every session is about trauma. It means I work in a way that takes into account how past experiences might affect you now, and I avoid doing anything that could make you feel unsafe or powerless.

In practice, that rests on a few principles.

**Safety.** Your physical and emotional safety comes first. That includes a calm space, predictable sessions and checking in with how you are going.

**Trust and transparency.** I explain what I am doing and why. There are no hidden agendas and no surprises.

**Choice.** You decide what we talk about and when. You can say no, pause or change direction at any time, and that is always respected.

**Collaboration.** We work together. You are the expert on your own life, and I bring skills and ideas to the conversation.

**Strengths.** I look for what has helped you survive and cope, not just what is hard. The ways you have protected yourself make sense, even if they are no longer working for you.

**Respect for who you are.** Your culture, identity, background and values shape how you experience the world, and they belong in the room.

## What it looks like in a session

You will never be pushed to tell your story before you are ready, and you may never need to go into detail at all. Plenty of healing happens without retelling hard experiences.

We might spend time on grounding and on understanding your stress responses, so you have tools to steady yourself when things feel too much. We will pay attention to your body as well as your thoughts. If something in a session feels overwhelming, we slow down.

Trauma-informed practice sits alongside the other approaches I use. It shapes how I use person-centred, strengths-based, CBT and ACT tools, so they are offered at your pace and in a way that feels safe.

## Do I need to have experienced trauma?

No. Trauma-informed counselling is simply good practice. Whether you are dealing with stress, a big life change, relationship difficulties or a feeling you cannot quite name, you deserve a space where you feel safe, respected and in control.
