Booking a first counselling session can feel like a big step. A lot of people put it off because they are not sure what will happen, what they will be asked, or whether they will know what to say. This post walks through what a first session with me looks like, so there are fewer unknowns.

## It starts before the first session

Before we book anything, I offer a free intro call. It is a short, relaxed chat where you can tell me a little about what is going on, ask questions and get a feel for whether we are a good fit. There is no pressure to book at the end of it. Some people know straight away, and some like to think it over.

If you do decide to go ahead, we book your first session. Sessions are 50 minutes, in person in the Illawarra or Greater Sydney, or online by video call.

## The first few minutes

Most first sessions start the same way: we sit down, I explain how counselling works and we go through confidentiality.

What you share with me stays between us. There are a small number of exceptions, and I will always explain them clearly at the start:

- if I believe you or someone else is at serious risk of harm
- if a child is at risk
- if a court orders me to release information

Knowing where the boundaries are helps you decide what you want to share and when.

## What we talk about

There is no script. I will usually ask what has brought you to counselling now, what has been happening for you and what you would like to be different. You can share as much or as little as you like.

Some people come in with a clear goal, like managing stress at work or getting through a big change. Others come in feeling stuck or overwhelmed and are not sure where to start. Both are completely fine. Part of my job is to help you find the thread.

I may also ask about what is already going well. My approach is strengths-based, which means I am just as interested in what helps you get through as in what is hard. Those strengths become the foundation for the work we do together.

## You set the pace

Counselling with me is person-centred. You decide what we talk about, how deep we go and how fast. If something feels like too much, you can tell me, and we will slow down or change direction.

I also work in a trauma-informed way. That means I put your sense of safety first, I explain what I am doing and why, and you always have a choice. You never have to share details of difficult experiences before you are ready, and you do not need to have "a trauma" to benefit from this approach.

## Will I leave with anything to do?

Sometimes. Depending on what we talk about, I might suggest a small thing to try between sessions. That could be noticing a pattern, trying a breathing exercise or writing down a few thoughts. I draw on approaches such as CBT and ACT when they fit, but nothing is compulsory and nothing is a test.

Other times, the first session is simply about being heard. That counts too.

## How you might feel afterwards

People leave a first session feeling all sorts of things: lighter, tired, relieved, unsure or a bit raw. All of that is normal. Talking about things you have been carrying can take energy. Be gentle with yourself for the rest of the day if you can.

## What happens next

At the end of the session we will talk about how it went for you and whether you would like to book another. Some people come weekly for a while, others fortnightly or less often. We will work out a rhythm that suits you, and you can change it at any time.

Sessions are $120 for 50 minutes. You do not need a referral. NDIS participants who are self-managed or plan-managed may be able to use their plan funding, and I can check this with you on your intro call.
